# Selected by Men editorial

Before drafting or revising a post, read `editorial.md` at the repo root.

**Skip sections 31–32 (Images / AI-generated images)** unless the user asks about images.

User instructions override the doc. The doc is a filter, not a straitjacket: apply the architecture to commercial guides and ranked lists; do not bolt a five-product ranking onto a short system (routine, capsule, kit) that is not a comparison.

## Always, any post

- Thesis first. Open on the problem, not the SKU list.
- Judgment with evidence. Specs mean something; they are not a dump.
- Why this product, and the tradeoff. Never all-perfect.
- Who it is for and who should skip, when the category has a skip.
- No fake hands-on: no “we tested / wore / used for months” unless that happened.
- Research-based unless the text says otherwise. Say so.
- Dates are real. No backdating. Last reviewed = when the work happened.
- Affiliate relationship in plain language. Amazon is the storefront, not independence theater.
- Conclusion compresses the decision. Never “we hope this helped.”
- Length until the decision is supported, then stop.

## Ranked lists / “best of”

Also follow `sbm-product-roundups`. Default architecture: problem → what matters → how we picked → what we did not evaluate → comparison table → products with roles → what we left out → who should buy which → decision.

H1 can be editorial; the query may live in `seoTitle` / title tag. Do not stamp every page “N Best X in 2026.”

## Not every post

A three-piece routine or capsule is a system, not a ranking. Give each piece a job and a catch. Do not invent a comparison table of unlike objects, “Best Overall” medals, or a 2,000-word methodology.

## After the draft

Run the Why? / credibility / “would we say this offline?” / “buy fewer things” checks in `editorial.md` §§40–44. Ignore the visual checklist unless images are in scope.
