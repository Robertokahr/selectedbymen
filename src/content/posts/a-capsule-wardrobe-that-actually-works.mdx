A capsule wardrobe is not a personality. It is a decision you make once so you do not make it every morning.

The internet version usually includes 40 items, a mood board, and a man who owns boat shoes in 2026. Ours is smaller. It assumes you go to an office some days, and you do not want your closet to require a curator.

## The rule

If it does not go with navy, white, brown, and denim, it is a statement. Statements are allowed in moderation. They are not the closet.

Build around:

- Two pairs of trousers (navy chinos, dark denim)
- Two Oxford or oxford-adjacent shirts (white, light blue)
- One merino crew
- One unstructured blazer in navy
- White leather sneakers and one brown leather shoe
- One belt that matches the brown shoes

That is already 80% of a calendar year. Outerwear is climate-specific; we will not pretend a peacoat is a national uniform.

## Sneakers: white leather, not “designer dad”

White sneakers are the modern dress shoe for men who do not work in finance theater. The silhouette that lasts is a court or Achilles shape — low, leather, minimal branding. The [adidas Stan Smith](https://www.adidas.com/us/stan_smith) is the version we keep coming back to.

What to look for:

- Leather you can clean with a cloth, not a knit that yellows in eight weeks.
- A cupsole that can be resoled or at least worn down slowly.
- A shape that still looks like a shoe next to tailored trousers.

Common Projects are the reference and the tax. Veja, Greats, Koio, and a careful Adidas Samba in white/white will get you most of the way. Avoid chunky “fashion” runners unless you are actually running.

Keep them white. Once a month: eraser, mild soap, patience. Yellowed foam is how a $150 pair becomes a $0 pair.

## Merino: the sweater that earns its keep

Cotton sweatshirts pill and bag. Cheap acrylic itches and looks like a hotel gym. **Merino** sits in the useful middle: temperature-regulating, odor-resistant, thin enough to go under a jacket. An [Icebreaker merino crew](https://www.icebreaker.com/) is the one we wear under a jacket.

Navy is the color. Grey is fine. Black merino photographs well and looks like a void in daylight. A crewneck, not a shawl collar, because you will actually layer it.

Fit: close in the shoulder, enough room that a shirt collar does not fight it. If the cuff looks like a sausage, size up.

Hand-wash or a wool cycle. Lay flat. This is not high maintenance. This is how the thing still looks like itself next winter.

## The belt you stop replacing

A bonded-leather belt from a 3-pack is how you get a cracked strap by August. **Full-grain leather**, 1.25 to 1.5 inches, a simple buckle that does not pretend to be jewelry.

Brown first. Black only if your shoes are black, which, for this closet, they mostly are not.

Match the metal to your watch if you care. If you do not care, pick brass and stop thinking about it. Punch extra holes if you must; better, buy the size that closes on the third hole from the end.

Hanks, Anson (if you want a removable buckle), or a no-name tannery belt with a listed leather type will all beat a department-store mystery hide.

## Putting it on

Monday: navy trousers, light blue shirt, merino if the office is cold, white sneakers, brown belt.

Saturday: dark denim, white tee or Oxford, same sneakers, same belt.

That repetition is the feature. People who dress well are often just people who stopped improvising.

When you add something new, it has to replace an item or fill a hole you can name (“rain,” “wedding,” “the gym”). “It was on sale” is not a hole.
