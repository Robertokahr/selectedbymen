## Before you buy: seven things that matter more than blade count

Six blades is not automatically closer than five. Geometry, how the head flexes, whether you press too hard, and how often you replace the cassette matter as much as the number on the box.

1. **Foil vs rotary.** Linear foil if you shave daily and like a blade-like pass. Circular rotary if your neck folds, hair grows in several directions, or you skip days.
2. **Beard type.** Dense, coarse stubble favors Panasonic. Mixed directions favor Philips.
3. **How often you shave.** Daily is Braun’s argument. Three to seven days is where Philips puts the i9000 on purpose.
4. **Skin.** Sensitive skin is not a brand. Technique, pressure, and frequency decide more than the logo. In general, the Series 9 Pro+ is the first foil we would point a reactive face at; the Arc6 is the last.
5. **Closeness versus comfort.** You can have a very close electric shave. You cannot always have it without some feel. Decide which one you will actually use at 7:10 a.m.
6. **Cost to own.** The handle is the down payment. Heads, cartridges, and proprietary chargers are the rest.
7. **Parts.** If the cassette is hard to find in two years, the “bargain” is a paperweight. Stick to Braun, Philips, and Panasonic for that reason.

## Foil vs rotary: which should you buy?

There is no universal winner. That is not a cop-out. Faces disagree, and so do comment threads.

**Foil** moves in a line. It is the easier jump from a safety razor or a cartridge, and the usual answer if you want the best foil shaver for a daily pass. Precision is high. Contours — under the nose, a folded neck — ask more of the head’s hinge. Braun and Panasonic live here.

**Rotary** uses three spinning heads. It follows a jaw because the heads can tilt independently. It is often kinder when hair grows in several directions and when you skip days. The stroke is circular; pressing harder does not help. Philips lives here.

If you already hate one system, do not “upgrade” inside it hoping the flagship will convert you. Buy the other system, or stay on a blade.

## Is a $400 electric shaver worth it?

Yes for some people. No for others.

If you shave daily, cartridges irritate you, and you will use the machine for four or five years, the cost-per-shave is not exotic — especially against a Fusion refill habit. If you already like a $100 rotary and you shave twice a week, spending $400 is a personality purchase, not a problem being solved.

Count the second bill: Braun cartridges and an 18-month cassette; Philips heads every two years; Panasonic oil, a new foil, and a charger that is not USB-C. The i9000 is the only one in this set that charges like a modern phone.

A cheap shaver at $80–$150 can be perfectly fine. The jump to this tier is closeness, sensors, wet/dry durability, and parts availability — not a different species of electricity.
